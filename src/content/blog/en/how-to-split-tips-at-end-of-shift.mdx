The argument almost never happens at 18:00. It happens at 23:40, when the last table has gone, two people already have their coats on, and somebody is dividing a pile of notes by headcount because that is faster than finding the rota.

Speed is the wrong optimisation. A split that cannot be checked will be checked later, in a worse mood. The UK code of practice is blunt about the point of a fair allocation: workers should be able to understand how tips were divided<SourceRef sources={frontmatter.sources} id="uk-tipping-code" />. End of shift is when that understanding is still cheap.

<AnswerBlock>
  Total the pool from the till, apply the written method, show the hours or
  points that produced each share, and share the breakdown before anyone in
  the pool leaves. The routine is the method. Doing it once, the same way,
  is what stops the next night from starting with yesterday's argument.
</AnswerBlock>

## Close while the till is still open

Three things decay the moment people walk out:

- **The cash.** Uncounted notes get rounded, borrowed, or "left in the tin".
- **The hours.** Clocked time is still on the system. Tomorrow it is a memory.
- **The chance to object.** A share seen on the night can be queried against the till. A share seen on Thursday cannot.

If a later shift takes over the room, split the period you have just closed. Combining lunch and dinner because it is easier is how a quiet lunch subsidises a busy night without anyone agreeing to that.

<TipBox title="One person calculates, another can see it">
  The closer does the arithmetic. Somebody else should be able to read the
  hours and the pool off the same screen. A split that only one person has
  ever seen is the one that gets relitigated.
</TipBox>

## What to do with the awkward remainder

Pools do not divide into clean coins. The options that fail are always the same: the closer keeps the cents, the till absorbs them, or they are handed to whoever complained.

Name a float. Put tonight's remainder in it, show it on the breakdown as "saved until the next split", and add it to tomorrow's pool. The amount is small. The fact that it is accounted for is not.
