An equal split is fair only when the shifts are. The moment one person works three hours and another works nine, dividing the pool by headcount is no longer a method — it is a dispute with extra arithmetic.

Splitting in proportion to hours worked is the usual way out. The hours are already on the rota. Anyone can check the sum. The UK's statutory code of practice lists hours worked during the period the tips were paid as a factor a fair allocation may rest on<SourceRef sources={frontmatter.sources} id="uk-tipping-code" />.

<AnswerBlock>
  Divide the pool by the total hours worked to get a per-hour rate, then
  multiply that rate by each person's hours. The shares always add back up,
  which is why the method survives being checked at the end of a shift.
</AnswerBlock>

## Why hours beat an equal split

Equal shares price presence: you were here, you get a cut. That is a reasonable rule for four people who all worked 18:00 to 23:00. It is not a reasonable rule for a lunch that ran long for two people and a dinner that started late for one.

Hours-weighted splitting prices time instead. A nine-hour shift takes three times the share of a three-hour shift, from the same pool, with no separate negotiation.

## The formula

<Formula
  label="Per-hour rate"
  note="Multiply the rate by each person's hours. If the hours were entered correctly, the shares add back up to the pool — that identity is the check."
>
  rate = total tips ÷ total hours worked
</Formula>

Two definitions have to be agreed once, in writing:

- **Which hours count.** Clocked hours on the floor is the usual choice. Unpaid breaks, pre-shift prep and a manager who is not in the pool need a yes or a no, not a vibe.
- **Which tips sit in the pool.** Cash, card, a service charge you have decided to share — list them. A split cannot be checked if the inputs drift.

  A list of final amounts invites the question "why". A rate of €10 an hour
  answers it before it is asked. If the team can see the hours and the rate,
  the rest is multiplication.
</TipBox>

## When hours are not enough

Hours treat every hour as equal. That is the point, and also the limit.

If a runner and a section lead should not earn the same hourly rate from the pool, do not abandon hours — weight them. A points system is hours with a multiplier: role points times hours, then divide the pool by total points. The hours are still the thing being counted; the points are a judgement about the role, written down once.

Hours also fail if the hours themselves are wrong. A split run on scheduled hours rather than clocked ones will look precise and still be unfair to whoever stayed late. Count what was actually worked.
