Tronc is an old catering word that still causes new hires to nod politely. It is not a different kind of tip. It is a name for a pool that someone is accountable for.

HMRC's description is the one that matters in the UK: a tronc is a separate, organised arrangement for pooling and distributing tips, gratuities and service charges, run independently of the employer, usually by a tronc master<SourceRef sources={frontmatter.sources} id="hmrc-troncs" />. Germany uses the same word, especially in casinos, for a common fund whose legal character depends on whether staff can enforce a share against the house.

<AnswerBlock>
  A tronc is a tip pool with a named person running it, independently of the
  employer. You need that structure when the tax rules care who controls the
  money. You need a written method and a checkable split whether you call the
  pot a tronc or not.
</AnswerBlock>

## A pool with a name on it

Every tronc is doing three ordinary jobs:

- collecting tips (and sometimes service charges) into one fund
- sharing that fund under a stated scheme
- keeping a record of both

The extra piece is **independence**. If the employer decides the shares, HMRC will not treat the arrangement as a tronc, however the staff rota spells the word. The tronc master applies a scheme; they are not a second manager quietly reallocating the night.

Set up a tronc when the country's rules make control of the money a tax question. In the UK, a genuine tronc can mean the employer is not liable for National Insurance on amounts they never decided<SourceRef sources={frontmatter.sources} id="hmrc-troncs" />. Qualifying tips still have to reach workers under the 2023 Act, tronc or not<SourceRef sources={frontmatter.sources} id="uk-tipping-act" />.

In Germany, *Trinkgeld* is tax-free only as a voluntary gift with no legal claim attached<SourceRef sources={frontmatter.sources} id="estg-3-51" />. A Tronc whose shares staff can enforce against the employer starts to look like wage. The word does not protect you; the arrangement does.

For a café that shares a jar by hours and writes that down, "tronc" is decoration. The written rule is the substance.

<WarningBox title="Do not borrow the tax answer from another country">
  UK National Insurance treatment does not travel. German § 3 Nr. 51 does not
  travel. If you operate in more than one place, name the arrangement locally
  and take advice locally. Copying a tronc clause from a London handbook into
  a Berlin contract is how a gift becomes payroll.
</WarningBox>

<TipBox title="The method still has to be a method">
  Hours, points or equal shares — pick one and apply it. A tronc master who
  "knows who worked hard tonight" is not administering a scheme. They are
  running a reputation system with cash attached.
</TipBox>
